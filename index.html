Reading Passage • Questions 1 to 4 Read carefully before answering Q1–Q4

Caution: Teens at Work

When teenagers look for a job, they often have just one thing in mind: money. Money for dates, or college, or even their family's rent and groceries. They aren't worried about long-term job security or climbing the career ladder. Most of them don't even think about job safety. But work-related injuries are among the biggest threats to their health. According to the National Institute for Occupational Safety and Health, more than 200,000 kids between the ages of 14 and 17 are hurt on the job every year in the United States, and more than 75,000 require emergency room treatment. Each year, nearly 70 kids under 18 die at work.

The types of injuries teens suffer are varied: Some burn themselves making French fries, some injure their backs while loading trucks, others get shot while working the night shift at a convenience store.

There are laws protecting them from some of the most dangerous and stressful tasks, but teens shouldn't assume their employers will know or follow these rules. Even large, well-known companies were caught in federal investigations of child labor law violations.

Teenage workers need to take charge of their own safety. They should insist on being trained and shouldn't hesitate to ask questions. Teens should also know that they have the right to refuse any task that seems unsafe. After all, some things really are more important than a paycheck.
